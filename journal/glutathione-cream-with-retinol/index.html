Can you use glutathione cream with retinol?

By Fair N Pink · Updated 5 October 2026

Short answer

Yes, glutathione cream and retinol can share one routine: use retinol at night (start twice a week) and the glutathione cream in the morning under sunscreen or on non-retinol nights. Skip retinol if pregnant or breastfeeding.

Yes, you can use a glutathione cream and a retinol product in the same routine, but most people do best by not layering them at the same time: keep retinol for the night and use the glutathione cream in the morning, or on the nights you skip retinol. Start retinol slowly, wear sunscreen every morning, and stop anything that stings or peels.

Why people ask this

Retinol (a form of vitamin A) is popular for smoother-looking skin and fine lines, while a glutathione cream is used for a brighter, more even-looking complexion. They do different jobs, so it is natural to want both. The worry is irritation: retinol can make skin dry, flaky and sensitive in the first weeks, and adding several active ingredients on top can make that worse, especially in Indian summers when sweat and heat already make skin react more easily.

How to start, step by step

  1. Settle one product first. If you are new to both, use the glutathione cream alone for two weeks so you know your skin is comfortable with it.
  2. Patch test the retinol on your inner arm or behind the ear for 24 hours.
  3. Begin with two retinol nights a week, a pea-sized amount for the whole face, on dry skin.
  4. Increase slowly to every other night only if there is no redness, burning or peeling.
  5. Keep sunscreen every single morning. This matters more than the order of your creams. See how to use sunscreen with a glutathione cream.

Can you layer them on the same night?

Some people with experienced, non-sensitive skin do apply retinol first and a moisturising cream on top after a few minutes. If you try this, do it only after several weeks of comfortable retinol use, keep both amounts small, and go back to alternate nights at the first sign of stinging. If your skin is sensitive, oily and acne-prone, or already irritated, alternate nights are the safer choice. There is no prize for using everything at once; consistency over weeks matters more.

Adjusting for Indian weather

In humid months, oily skin may prefer a lighter layer of cream and fewer retinol nights. In dry North Indian winters, retinol tends to feel harsher, so add a plain moisturiser on retinol nights and avoid hot water on your face. During long outdoor days or travel in strong sun, it is sensible to pause retinol for a few days and keep your morning cream and sunscreen going.

What to avoid in the same routine

  • Face scrubs, peel-off masks and strong exfoliating acids on retinol nights.
  • Retinol on freshly waxed, threaded or sunburnt skin.
  • Any cream that does not list its ingredients or promises a dramatic change in days. Never use products with steroids, mercury or hydroquinone unless a doctor prescribes them.

Who should skip retinol or ask a doctor first

Do not use retinol if you are pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding, unless your doctor says otherwise. If you use a prescription retinoid (such as tretinoin or adapalene) or any acne medicine, ask your dermatologist before adding other active creams. Anyone with eczema, rosacea, melasma or very sensitive skin should also see a dermatologist rather than experimenting.

FAQ

Which goes first, retinol or glutathione cream?

On a night when you use both, retinol goes first on dry skin, and the cream goes on top a few minutes later. In the simpler split routine, they never meet: the cream in the morning, retinol at night.

Can I use glutathione cream every day if I use retinol?

Yes, the cream can be used daily in the morning while retinol stays at night. See daily use, morning or night.

Will using both give faster results?

Not necessarily. Skin changes slowly, and irritation can set you back. Give any routine four to eight weeks with daily sunscreen, and expect results to differ from person to person.

My skin is peeling. What should I do?

Pause retinol and other actives, use only a gentle cleanser, a plain moisturiser and sunscreen until your skin calms down, and restart retinol less often. If burning, swelling or rash appears, stop and see a dermatologist. Also read side effects and safety.

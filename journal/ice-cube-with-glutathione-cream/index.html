Can you use ice cubes with glutathione cream?

By Fair N Pink · Updated 5 October 2026

Short answer

Yes, briefly. Wrap an ice cube in a cotton cloth, glide it over clean skin for about a minute, pat dry, wait a couple of minutes, then apply the cream. Ice goes first, never over the cream, and sensitive or very dry skin should skip it.

Yes, you can use an ice cube with glutathione cream: wrap the ice in a thin cotton cloth, glide it over the face for about a minute, pat the skin dry, then apply the cream. Ice comes first, on clean skin, and never directly after the cream.

Face icing is popular in India, especially in summer and before events. Cold briefly tightens the look of the skin and can make a puffy morning face look fresher for a while. It is a quick comfort step, not a brightening treatment, and it does not change what your cream does.

1. What icing can and cannot do

What it can doWhat it cannot do
PuffinessMake a puffy morning face look calmer for a short timeFix puffiness from poor sleep or salty food for the whole day

2. The right order

  1. Wash your face with a mild face wash.
  2. Wrap one ice cube in a thin, clean cotton cloth or handkerchief. Never put bare ice on the skin.
  3. Glide it gently over the forehead, cheeks, chin and under-eye area for 30 seconds to 1 minute in total. Keep it moving; do not hold it in one spot.
  4. Pat the face dry with a soft towel and wait 2 to 3 minutes for the skin to return to normal temperature.
  5. Apply a thin layer of the glutathione cream on the face and neck.
  6. In the morning, finish with sunscreen; if you ice before going out, sunscreen goes on after the cream.

For the daily cream steps, see how to use the cream, and for sunscreen layering read glutathione cream with sunscreen.

3. Why not ice after the cream?

  • Rubbing ice over the cream wipes much of it off and leaves water on the face.
  • Melted water dilutes the cream, so it spreads unevenly.
  • Cold, wet skin with a cream on top can feel uncomfortable, especially on sensitive skin.

If you want a cooling feel after the cream, keep the jar closed in a cool cupboard, not the freezer, and apply as usual.

5. Ice cube add-ins: what is fine and what to skip

  • Plain water: the safest choice.
  • Cucumber juice or plain rose water: fine for most skin if patch tested; see rose water with glutathione cream and glutathione cream with cucumber.
  • Green tea: fine once cooled and strained; see glutathione cream with green tea.
  • Lemon or tomato juice: skip; acids sting and can irritate, more so on cold, freshly rubbed skin.
  • Coffee or turmeric cubes: can stain and add little.

6. Who should skip icing

  • Sensitive or easily red skin, rosacea or broken veins on the cheeks: cold can make redness worse.
  • Very dry winter skin or skin that is peeling.
  • Freshly waxed, threaded or shaved skin, or skin that is broken.
  • Active, painful acne: brief cold can feel soothing, but rubbing spreads irritation; ask a dermatologist about painful pimples.
  • Anyone whose skin goes white, numb or very red with cold: stop at once.

8. When to see a dermatologist

Stop icing and see a dermatologist if your skin stays red, blotchy, numb or itchy after cold, if you get a rash, or if puffiness under the eyes is there every day or comes with swelling elsewhere. Patch test the cream first, and read glutathione cream for sensitive skin if you react easily.

FAQ

Should I use ice before or after glutathione cream?

Before. Ice on clean skin, pat dry, wait a couple of minutes, then apply the cream.

How long should I keep ice on my face?

About 30 seconds to 1 minute in total, wrapped in a cloth and kept moving. Longer is not better.

Can I rub ice on my face every day?

A short, wrapped icing once a day is fine for most oily or normal skin in summer. Skip it on dry, sensitive or broken skin.

Does ice make skin fair?

No. Ice does not change your natural skin colour or remove tan. It gives a short-lived fresh, less puffy look.

Can I put glutathione cream in ice cubes?

No. Freezing and melting changes the cream, and you lose control of how much you apply. Use plain ice first and the cream after.

Can you use rice water with glutathione cream?

By Fair N Pink · Updated 5 October 2026

Short answer

Yes. Splash or pat fresh, cooled rice water on clean skin, let it dry, then apply a thin layer of the glutathione cream, with sunscreen every morning. Make a fresh batch every day or two, never mix it into the jar, and remember rice water does not fade dark spots.

2. How to make rice water at home

  1. Rinse half a cup of plain rice once in clean water and throw that water away. It carries dust.
  2. Soak the rice in about a cup of clean water for 20 to 30 minutes and swirl it now and then. The water turns cloudy.
  3. Strain the water into a clean glass bottle or bowl. Cook the rice as usual.
  4. Use it the same day, or keep it in the fridge with a closed lid and throw it away after a day or two.
  5. Throw it away earlier if it smells sour, turns slimy or looks different.

Boiled rice water (the starch you drain after cooking) also works if you dilute it with an equal amount of clean water and let it cool completely.

3. The right order with the cream

  1. Patch test the rice water on your inner arm or behind the ear and check the next day.
  2. Wash your face with a mild face wash and pat it dry.
  3. Splash or pat on rice water with clean palms or a fresh cotton pad. Keep it out of the eyes.
  4. Let it dry for a minute or two. You can rinse it off with plain water if it feels tight.
  5. Apply a thin layer of the glutathione cream, away from the eyes and lips.
  6. Sunscreen every morning, SPF 30 or higher, if you use the routine in the day.

Watery steps go first and creams after, so the cream can sit on top and keep skin comfortable. For the daily cream steps, read how to use the cream.

4. Fermented rice water: go slow

Many online recipes leave rice water out for a day or two to ferment. Fermented rice water is more sour, smells strong and can sting, and in warm Indian weather it can grow the wrong kind of germs quickly. If you want to try it, keep it in the fridge, use it diluted, never past two days, and stop at the first sign of itching or bumps. For most people, fresh rice water is the simpler and safer choice.

6. Who should be careful

  • Dry skin. Rice water can leave a tight feeling, especially in winter. Follow with the cream straight away; see glutathione cream for dry skin.
  • Acne-prone skin. A starchy layer left on all day can feel sticky; rinse it off after a few minutes. See glutathione cream for acne prone skin.
  • Sensitive skin that reacts easily; patch test first and see glutathione cream for sensitive skin.
  • Broken skin, or skin right after threading, waxing or bleach.

7. Rice water for glow: an honest view

Rice water is often shared online as a "glass skin" or glow secret. It can make skin feel soft and look fresher for a while, which is a fine reason to enjoy it. There is no reliable evidence that it fades dark spots or tan. Those look lighter slowly, over weeks, with sunscreen every morning and steady care; read how to reduce dark spots on face and glutathione cream with sunscreen.

See a dermatologist if your skin itches, burns or breaks into bumps after rice water, if a rash spreads, or if dark patches on the cheeks keep spreading (this can be melasma, which needs a doctor).

FAQ

Can I apply glutathione cream after rice water?

Yes. Let the rice water dry for a minute or two, then apply a thin layer of the cream.

Can I mix rice water into my glutathione cream?

No. Water in the jar invites germs and spoils the cream. Keep rice water as a separate step.

Can I use rice water on my face every day?

Most people can use fresh rice water daily as a splash. Make a new batch every day or two and stop if your skin feels dry or itchy.

Is fermented rice water better than fresh?

Not necessarily. Fermented rice water spoils and stings more easily, especially in warm weather. Fresh rice water is the safer choice.

Does rice water with glutathione cream make skin fair?

No. Neither changes your natural skin colour. Used with sunscreen, the cream can help tan and marks look lighter over weeks.

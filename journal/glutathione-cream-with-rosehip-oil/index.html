Can you use rosehip oil with glutathione cream?

By Fair N Pink · Updated 5 October 2026

Short answer

Yes. Apply the glutathione cream first, wait a few minutes, then press 1 to 2 drops of pure rosehip oil on dry or rough areas at night, with sunscreen every morning. Store the oil away from heat and throw it away if it smells sharp. Oily skin in humid weather usually does not need it.

Yes, you can use rosehip oil with glutathione cream: apply the cream first, wait a few minutes, then press 1 to 2 drops of pure rosehip oil onto dry or rough areas at night. Do not mix the oil into the jar, and wear sunscreen every morning.

Rosehip oil is pressed from the seeds of wild rose plants and is sold in India mostly in small dropper bottles as a face oil. It is a light, "dry-feeling" oil that sinks in faster than coconut, castor or olive oil, which is why people with normal and combination skin often like it. It is also one of the quickest oils to go stale once opened, so how you buy and store it matters.

2. The right order

  1. Wash your face with a mild face wash and pat it dry.
  2. Apply a thin layer of the glutathione cream on the face and neck.
  3. Wait 3 to 5 minutes so the cream settles.
  4. Put 1 to 2 drops of rosehip oil on your fingertips and warm them between your fingers.
  5. Press gently onto dry or rough areas: cheeks, around the nose, chin and jawline. Avoid the eyelids.
  6. Use it at night only. In the morning, a light moisturiser and sunscreen are enough.

Cream first, oil second: the cream sits directly on clean skin, and the thin oil layer on top helps slow water loss overnight. For the daily cream steps, read how to use the cream.

4. Rosehip oil is not "natural retinol"

You will often see rosehip oil sold as a natural alternative to retinol. That claim goes too far. Rosehip oil is rich in fatty acids such as linoleic acid and also contains small amounts of carotenoids, which give it its orange colour, but a face oil is not the same as a measured retinol product. Use it for comfort and softness, not as a way to fade marks. If you are thinking about retinol, read glutathione cream with retinol first.

5. How to buy and store rosehip oil

  • Read the ingredient list: it should say rosehip seed oil (often written as Rosa canina or Rosa rubiginosa seed oil) and little else.
  • Skip bottles with fragrance, "parfum" or added essential oils if you want it for the face.
  • Buy a small, dark glass bottle. You only need a couple of drops a night, and a big bottle may go stale before you finish it.
  • Store it capped, away from sun and heat. In a hot Indian summer, a cool cupboard is better than a sunny dressing table or a bathroom shelf near the geyser.
  • Smell it before each use. Fresh oil smells mild and slightly earthy. A sharp, crayon-like or fishy smell means it has gone rancid; throw it away.
  • Note the date you opened it and follow the use-by advice on the bottle.

6. Rosehip vs other oils

If you already have an oil at home, you do not need to buy rosehip. Jojoba is similar in weight and keeps better; argan is a little richer; almond and olive oil are richer still and suit very dry winter skin; coconut and castor oil are heavy and better kept to lips, heels and very dry patches. Compare them in jojoba oil with glutathione cream, argan oil with glutathione cream and almond oil with glutathione cream.

7. What not to do

  • Do not mix rosehip oil into the cream jar. It changes the cream and can spoil it.
  • Do not apply it under the cream. The oil layer can stop the cream from sitting on the skin.
  • Do not use it on freshly waxed, threaded or bleached skin for a day or two; see face cream after facial, threading and waxing.
  • Do not use it in the morning under sunscreen on oily skin; it can make sunscreen slide off with sweat.
  • Do not expect it to fade spots or tan. Daily sunscreen does far more; see glutathione cream with sunscreen.

9. When to see a dermatologist

Stop the oil and see a dermatologist if your skin turns red, itchy or swollen, if you get many new bumps or whiteheads, or if dryness comes with cracking, scaling or patches that do not settle with a simple moisturiser. Patch test any new oil on the jawline for 2 to 3 nights first, and read glutathione cream for sensitive skin if you react easily. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor before starting new skincare products.

FAQ

Can I apply rosehip oil after glutathione cream?

Yes. Apply the cream first, wait a few minutes, then press 1 to 2 drops of pure rosehip oil on dry areas at night.

Is rosehip oil good for oily skin?

It is a light oil, but oily skin usually does not need extra oil, especially in humid weather. Skip it, or use one drop only on dry patches.

Can rosehip oil replace my glutathione cream?

No. Rosehip oil helps skin feel soft; the cream is the step meant to help skin look brighter and more even over weeks with sunscreen.

How do I know if my rosehip oil has gone bad?

A sharp, crayon-like or fishy smell, or a darker colour than when you bought it, means it has gone rancid. Throw it away and do not use it on the face.

Does rosehip oil with glutathione cream make skin fair?

No. Neither changes your natural skin colour. Used with sunscreen, the cream can help tan and marks look lighter over weeks.

Glutathione cream for oily skin: how to use it without a greasy, shiny face

By Fair N Pink · Updated 5 October 2026

Short answer

Yes, oily skin can use glutathione cream: apply a very thin layer (about half a pea) on a freshly washed face, mostly at night, and keep mornings to a light layer or just sunscreen. Use less product rather than skipping moisture.

Yes, oily skin can use a glutathione cream: apply a very thin layer, mostly at night, on a freshly washed face, and keep the morning light with just the cream (or nothing) under sunscreen. Oily skin still needs moisture; the trick is using less product, not skipping it.

Most complaints from oily skin, such as a sticky feel, midday shine or small bumps, come from using too much cream, layering several heavy products, or applying it on a face that still has sweat and dirt on it. The steps below fix those three things first.

1. Why oily skin still needs a cream

Washing too often or skipping moisturiser can leave the skin feeling tight, and many people then wash even more. A thin layer of a light cream keeps the surface comfortable so you are not stuck in that cycle. Oily skin also tans and gets dark marks after pimples, which is where a cream made for brighter-looking, more even skin can fit, used together with daily sunscreen.

2. How much to apply

On oily skin, half of a pea-sized amount is usually enough for the whole face. Warm it between two fingertips, dot it on the cheeks, forehead and chin, and spread it thin. If your face still looks shiny after five minutes, you used too much; use less the next day rather than wiping it off.

Combination skin can use a little more on the cheeks and very little on the forehead and nose, where oil is usually highest.

4. Step by step at night

  1. Wash with a mild face wash and lukewarm water. If you wore sunscreen or makeup all day, wash twice.
  2. Pat dry with a clean towel and wait a minute so the skin is not dripping.
  3. Apply half a pea-sized amount of cream in a thin layer over the face and neck.
  4. Leave it on overnight. Change your pillow cover often; old oil and sweat on it do not help.

If you use a separate pimple treatment from a dermatologist, ask them where the cream should go in your routine.

5. Sunscreen for oily skin

Sun is a main reason tan and dark marks stay longer, so sunscreen every morning matters more than any cream. Oily skin usually prefers a gel or fluid sunscreen that sets without a heavy feel. Use enough to cover the whole face and neck, and reapply if you are outdoors for long. See glutathione cream with sunscreen for the right order.

6. Mistakes that make oily skin worse

  • Washing four or five times a day: twice is enough; on very sweaty days, rinse with plain water in between.
  • Thick layers: more cream does not mean faster results; it only adds shine and can clog pores.
  • Stacking many actives: a strong scrub, a toner and several serums plus a cream can irritate oily skin too.
  • Applying on a sweaty face: wipe or rinse sweat first, especially after travel.
  • Picking pimples: it leaves darker marks. Read glutathione cream for pimple marks for what a cream can and cannot do for them.

8. When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if you have many painful or deep pimples, pimples that leave pitted scars, sudden heavy oiliness with other changes such as hair growth or irregular periods, or if your skin reacts to every product you try. These need proper diagnosis, not a cosmetic cream.

FAQ

Will glutathione cream make oily skin more oily?

A thin layer should not. If your face looks greasy, reduce the amount, use it only at night, and keep the morning to sunscreen.

Can I use it in summer if my skin is oily?

Yes. In hot, humid months many people with oily skin use the cream only at night and wear sunscreen in the day.

Can it cause pimples?

Any cream can block pores if you apply too much on skin that is already congested. Use a thin layer on a clean face, and stop if you get new bumps where you apply it.

Do I need a separate moisturiser?

Usually not on oily skin. The cream is enough; add a plain moisturiser only if your skin feels tight, for example in winter or after a long day in AC.

How long before I notice a difference?

Skin changes slowly and results differ from person to person. Give it several weeks of daily use with sunscreen before you judge it.

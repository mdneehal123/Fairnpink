Can you use petroleum jelly with glutathione cream?

By Fair N Pink · Updated 5 October 2026

Short answer

Yes, at night: apply the glutathione cream first, wait a few minutes, then press a thin layer of plain petroleum jelly only on dry areas and lips. Never put the jelly under the cream or mix it into the jar, and oily or acne-prone skin can usually skip it.

Yes, you can use petroleum jelly with glutathione cream: apply the cream first on clean skin, wait a few minutes, then press a thin layer of plain petroleum jelly over dry areas, at night only. The jelly goes on top, never under the cream and never mixed into the jar.

Plain petroleum jelly is a common winter staple in Indian homes for chapped lips, cracked heels and dry hands. It works as a seal on top of the skin, so it can help dry patches feel more comfortable. It has no brightening ingredients of its own, and it does not remove dark spots or tan.

2. The right order at night

  1. Wash your face with a mild face wash and pat it dry.
  2. Apply a thin layer of the glutathione cream, away from the eyes and lips.
  3. Wait 5 to 10 minutes so the cream settles.
  4. Take a rice-grain amount of plain petroleum jelly, warm it between your fingertips and press it only on dry areas: flaky cheeks, the sides of the nose, the chin.
  5. Use a separate, clean fingertip for your lips. The jelly is fine on lips; the cream is not.
  6. In the morning, wash your face, then use a light moisturiser and sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher.

Putting the jelly first would block the cream from reaching the skin, which is why it always goes last. For the daily cream steps, read how to use the cream.

3. Is "slugging" a good idea?

"Slugging" means covering the whole face with a layer of petroleum jelly overnight. It can help very dry, flaky skin in a cold, dry winter. But a sealed layer can also make any active cream underneath feel stronger, and it can trap sweat in humid weather. If you want to try it, start with dry areas only, two or three nights a week, and stop if you notice stinging, redness or new bumps. Most people in warm Indian cities do not need it at all.

5. Who should skip it on the face

  • Oily or acne-prone skin. A heavy seal can trap oil and sweat; a thin layer of cream is enough. See glutathione cream for oily skin.
  • Humid weather. In coastal cities or the monsoon, it usually feels sticky. Keep it for dry winter nights.
  • Skin with active pimples, or skin that is red and irritated.
  • Anyone sleeping on a pillow they do not want stained. Use a thin layer or an old pillowcase.

7. Petroleum jelly for dark spots: an honest view

Petroleum jelly is sometimes shared online as an overnight trick for glowing skin or lighter spots. It can make very dry skin look smoother and less dull in the morning, which is useful in winter. It does not fade dark spots or tan. Those look lighter slowly, over weeks, with sunscreen every morning and steady care; read glutathione cream in winter and how to reduce dark spots on face.

9. When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if dryness cracks, bleeds or itches badly, if new bumps keep appearing after using jelly, if your skin burns after the cream, or if dark patches on the cheeks keep spreading (this can be melasma, which needs a doctor).

FAQ

Can I apply petroleum jelly after glutathione cream?

Yes, at night. Wait a few minutes after the cream, then press a thin layer only on dry areas.

Can I apply petroleum jelly before glutathione cream?

No. The jelly forms a seal, so the cream on top would not reach your skin. Cream first, jelly last.

Can I mix petroleum jelly into my glutathione cream?

No. Keep them separate so the cream stays as it was made and you control where the jelly goes.

Is petroleum jelly good for oily skin?

Usually not on the face. It can trap oil and sweat. Oily skin is fine with just a thin layer of cream.

Does petroleum jelly with glutathione cream make skin fair?

No. Neither changes your natural skin colour. Used with sunscreen, the cream can help tan and marks look lighter over weeks.
